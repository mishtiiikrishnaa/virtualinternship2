I planned a 30-hour programming fundamentals module for adults who have never written code, taught in Python through the browser so nothing needs installing. The plan covers who the learners are, what public research says about common beginner challenges and what helps, seven measurable learning outcomes mapped to Bloom levels and to modules and assessments, ten modules from thinking in steps through variables, choices, loops, functions, lists, files, and reading errors, ending in a personal mini-project, plus teaching methods, assessment, resources, timeline, risks, and success measures. It includes six diagrams: a curriculum flowchart, a learner journey, a Gantt timeline, a session shape, an assessment map, and a resource chart. Dates in the timeline are planned from a 5 November 2026 baseline, the first working day after the 4 November 2026 report date, skipping weekends and govind jayanti on 25 november 2026, with 120 planned hours matching the resource table. Key decisions were Python over Scratch and JavaScript for readability and browser access, worked examples and Parsons problems to lower cognitive load, frequent short feedback, and a capstone rubric that rewards explanation as well as a working program. The document uses plain language throughout, with a glossary, so a non-technical reader can follow every section without prior knowledge.
 Planning started from the internship offer received on 2 november 2026 and covers the internship period of 2 to 30 november 2026, with the report prepared on 4 november 2026. Every milestone in the timeline is clearly labelled planned, the due dates rise in order, and the plan is designed so learners gain confidence early, practise in pairs, and finish with an artifact they can explain to others.
