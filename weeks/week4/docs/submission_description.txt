Week 4: Reflection, Evaluation, and Future Planning

This final week concluded the four-virtual-internship on a programming fundamentals curriculum for non-programmers. The curriculum teaches Python fundamentals through browser-based execution via Google Colab, eliminating local installation. Ten modules cover core concepts from computational thinking and variables to functions, collections, file handling, and debugging, building toward a capstone mini-project. The Week 3 interactive module provides browser-based interactive visualisations, Parsons problems, debugging challenges, predict-the-output exercises, hands-on coding, and a guided final project.

The Week 4 assignment required reflection, evaluation, and future planning. The evaluation methodology included a comprehensive curriculum audit, functional testing of interactive modules, instructional evaluation using a defined rubric, and quantitative analysis. Key findings: 100% learning objective coverage across all 12 measurable objectives; 100% assessment coverage (every objective has at least one associated assessment); 20 quiz questions across 10 modules; 13 activities of five types; 19 functional test cases for the interactive module; and 30 teaching hours distributed across 20 sessions and 6 block types. Strengths include full objective coverage, strong activity-assessment alignment, progressive concept sequencing, balanced block time allocation with most time for learner practice, a comprehensive misconception catalogue, consistent session structure, capstone integration, and single-source-of-truth design. Key weaknesses include the absence of empirical evaluation with actual learners, the research-unbacked capstone pass mark, the interactive module's untested usability with beginners, constrained assessment diversity, and accessibility limitations.

Response to Week 3 evaluator feedback (score 59.5/100: "lacks specific data; more actionable suggestions needed") involved adding quantitative data, developing a functional test suite, constructing a learning objective traceability matrix, and creating a prioritised improvement roadmap with short-term, medium-term, and long-term classifications. The personal reflection covers skills developed in curriculum design and technical implementation with Pyodide and JavaScript; challenges in communicating programming concepts; lessons from evaluator feedback; strengths (complete curriculum, consistent structure, test suite, assessment instruments, consistent figures); weaknesses (no empirical learner evaluation, accessibility gaps); how the project could have been approached differently; and how the experience relates to future educational technology work. The roadmap classifies 14 recommendations across short-term (immediate documentation, test suite), medium-term (adaptive exercises, additional assessments, accessibility), and long-term (research-oriented features) priorities.

The proposed empirical learner evaluation design includes 30-50 non-programming adult participants, pre-test/post-test assessments, task completion measurements, and success criteria such as 60%+ positive learning gain on 9+ of 12 objectives.
